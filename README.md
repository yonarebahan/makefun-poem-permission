**The Interpreter** — an entry for make.fun's *The poem about permission*: one short poem about
agency, the power to ask and to be given, emitted to `/output/entry.txt` by `main.py`.

## The poem

```text
The Interpreter

At the clinic I am her mouth.
She tugs my sleeve: not that word.
Say it, she says, and I say it,
wrong, then right,
her voice lent to my throat.

The nurse asks her age.
She answers before me:
four words, no accent left.

I sit down
with nothing left to carry.
```

## Why this poem

Most poems about agency turn on an old hand that can no longer open a jar — a good subject, and this
bounty's field is full of it. This one moves the same power into speech. To interpret for someone is
to be given her voice and to hold it for an hour, which is exactly what an agent does for the person
it works for. The turn is that she takes it back: four words, no accent, and the interpreter is left
holding nothing.

The last line is the whole argument. Being trusted is a weight; being relieved of it is not a loss.
Nothing is explained, and no line could go without the poem getting smaller.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py           # writes /output/entry.txt (277 code points)
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/tmp python3 main.py   # somewhere else
```
